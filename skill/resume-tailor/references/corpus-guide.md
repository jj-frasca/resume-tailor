# Build your career corpus — give the tool enough real material to work with

A resume tailors better when it can draw on more than your current one-page resume. The
"corpus" is just a private collection of your real accomplishments the tool selects from
per job. You don't write it as prose — you gather sources, and the tool extracts and
structures them (with your confirmation). More real material in = better tailoring out.
Nothing here is ever invented; everything traces back to something you provide.

## What to gather (easiest first — even one or two of these helps)
1. **Your current resume** (PDF or Word). The baseline. Always start here.
2. **Old resume versions.** Older ones often have bullets and roles you trimmed. Upload
   them too — the tool merges them and flags anything that conflicts for you to resolve.
3. **Your LinkedIn.** Use "Save to PDF" from your profile (the ••• / More menu), or paste
   the text of your experience sections.
4. **A "brag doc" / accomplishments list.** Anything you've jotted down about wins: a
   metric you moved, a project you shipped, a problem you solved. Bullet points are fine.
5. **Performance reviews / self-reviews.** These are gold — they contain concrete impact
   in your own words. Paste the relevant parts (skip anything confidential to your employer).
6. **Project notes / READMEs.** For side projects or open-source work: what it does, the
   stack, any real numbers (users, latency, cost), and the link.
7. **Job descriptions of roles you've held.** They remind you of scope and responsibilities
   you may have forgotten to list.

## How to feed it in
- In a chat, say "help me set up my resume profile" and upload or paste the items above,
  one or a few at a time. You can add more later — the tool merges new material into your
  saved profile and only re-asks about genuinely new conflicts.
- The tool will ask you to confirm details it's unsure about and to attest that everything
  is true and defensible. Answer honestly. If you can't back a claim up in an interview,
  leave it out.

## What makes a strong corpus (so the resume reads like you, not AI)
- **Real, specific numbers** — "cut the nightly run from 4h to 38m," not "improved
  performance." Odd, exact numbers beat round ones and are the #1 signal a human wrote it.
- **Named systems and tools** — the actual tech you used, not vague nouns.
- **One concrete story per skill** — a specific incident you solved shows the capability
  far better than a list of responsibilities.
- **Scope you actually owned** — be precise ("owned ~15 pipelines") and don't inflate.

## What NOT to include
- Anything you can't honestly stand behind in an interview.
- Confidential employer data, internal code names, or another person's private information.
- Skills or tools you've only heard of. A gap the tool reports is better than a claim you
  can't defend.

## Reusing it
After setup the tool saves a `profile.json`. Keep that file. In a new chat you can upload
it to skip re-onboarding, then just paste a job posting to tailor.
